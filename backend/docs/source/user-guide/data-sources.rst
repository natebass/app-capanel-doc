Where the Data Comes From
================================================================

Everything on this site is published by the California Department of Education.
Nothing is estimated, adjusted or re-scored.

The source
----------

Results come from the **research files** that accompany the state's public
reports at `caaspp-elpac.ets.org <https://caaspp-elpac.ets.org/caaspp/Default>`_.
Those files carry the same figures as the state's own "Test Results" pages, in
bulk, for every school, district and county in California.

Two programs are covered:

**CAASPP** — Smarter Balanced English language arts/literacy and mathematics,
the California Science Test, the California Spanish Assessment, and the
alternate assessments.

**ELPAC** — the English language proficiency assessments taken by students
whose primary language is other than English.

The full list of tests, and what each one measures, is in
:doc:`../data/assessments`.

What the numbers mean
---------------------

Percentages are shown exactly as the state published them.  Where a test
publishes a combined "met or exceeded the standard" figure — Smarter Balanced
and the science test do — that figure is used directly.  Where it does not, the
site adds up the levels the state counts as meeting the standard and labels the
result *derived*, so it is clear which is which.

Blanks are not zeroes
---------------------

A dash means one of two things, and the site says which:

**Withheld.**  The state publishes no results for any group of fewer than 11
students, so that individual students cannot be identified.

**Not reported.**  The figure does not apply.  Most often this is the mean scale
score on an "All Grades" row: scale scores are set separately for each grade and
cannot be averaged across them, so the state publishes none.

Comparisons to make carefully
-----------------------------

* **2019–20 is missing.**  Testing was suspended statewide because of COVID-19.
* **The California Spanish Assessment changed in 2024–25.**  Its blueprint and
  scale were redesigned, so earlier scores are not comparable. The site says so
  on any trend that crosses the change.
* **The Spanish assessment is optional** and is built on content that is not
  taught everywhere, so the state recommends against comparing schools on it.
* **Charter schools are counted in two places.**  Independent charters are
  reported as their own districts *and* as schools. The "Schools included"
  filter rebuilds charter-only and non-charter-only figures from the school
  rows; those are marked as recalculated.

The full set of cautions is in :doc:`../data/interpreting-results`.

Keeping it current
------------------

New administrations are published by the state once a year, usually in the
autumn.  Loading them into this site is a matter of putting the new research
files where the importer can see them — see
:doc:`../developer-guide/importing-research-files`.
